# Webflow 301 Redirect Generator

Paste your old URLs or sitemap and get a Webflow-ready 301 redirect map in seconds. New paths are suggested for you, chains and duplicates are flagged, and you can export a CSV.

## How to use this tool

1. **Paste your old URLs**: One per line, full URLs or paths. You can also paste your whole sitemap.xml and the tool pulls out every URL.
2. **Set your URL rules**: Choose how new paths are suggested: no trailing slashes, no .html, lowercase, dated posts into a CMS collection, plus your own folder rules.
3. **Review and edit**: Edit any new path in the table. Duplicates, redirect chains and loops are flagged so you can fix them before launch.
4. **Export to Webflow**: Download the CSV or copy it, then add each row in Webflow under Site settings, Publishing, 301 redirects.

### What is a 301 redirect in Webflow?

A 301 redirect permanently sends visitors and search engines from an old URL to a new one. In Webflow you add them under Site settings, Publishing, 301 redirects, with the old path on the left and the new path on the right.

### Do I need 301 redirects when I migrate to Webflow?

Yes, for every URL that changes. Without them, old links and Google results lead to 404 pages and the rankings those pages earned are lost. URLs that stay exactly the same don't need a redirect.

### Can one Webflow redirect cover many URLs?

Yes. Webflow supports pattern redirects with capture groups, such as /(\d{4})/(\d{2})/(.*) to /blog/%3, which moves every dated WordPress post into a blog collection with one rule. The tool suggests this when it finds dated posts.

### What are redirect chains and why avoid them?

A chain is when a redirect points to a URL that redirects again. Each extra hop slows the page and can dilute SEO value, so every old URL should redirect straight to its final new path. The tool flags chains and loops.

### Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. The tool runs entirely in your browser. Your URLs are never sent to a server.
