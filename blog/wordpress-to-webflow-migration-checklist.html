The WordPress to Webflow Migration Checklist

Webflow Migration Expert · 8 min read

If you're moving off WordPress, the design and the hosting are the easy part to picture. The part that actually goes wrong is SEO: pages that quietly stop ranking a few weeks after launch because a URL changed, a redirect was missed, or a meta description didn't come along for the ride.

Here's the checklist I run through on every WordPress migration, in order.

1. Full URL inventory

Before anything moves, every indexed URL on the site gets pulled, not just the pages in your nav menu. That means blog posts, category pages, tag pages, and anything Google has crawled and ranked, even the ones you forgot existed.

2. A 301 redirect map

Every URL that changes on the new site gets mapped to its Webflow equivalent with a 301 redirect, before launch, not after someone notices traffic dropped. This is the single most common thing DIY migrations skip.

3. Metadata carried over exactly

Title tags, meta descriptions, and header structure (H1, H2, H3) get preserved or improved, never left blank on the new build. Google re-indexes based on what it can see, not what used to be there.

4. Content and media migration

Blog posts, images, and alt text move into Webflow's CMS with the same structure they had before, so nothing looks like it was dumped in without formatting.

  • Internal links checked and repointed to the new URLs
  • Image alt text preserved for accessibility and image search
  • XML sitemap regenerated and resubmitted to Search Console

5. A staging launch, not a live one

The new site gets built and tested on a staging URL first. DNS only cuts over once redirects are verified and the site's been checked end to end, so your current site keeps working the entire time you're waiting.

6. Post-launch monitoring

After launch, Search Console gets watched for crawl errors and dropped pages for the first few weeks, not just handed off and forgotten. Catching a missed redirect in week one is a five-minute fix. Catching it in month three is a lost quarter of traffic.
